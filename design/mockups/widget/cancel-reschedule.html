In flow

Change of mind, three ways

Left: a clean cancel. Middle: a clean reschedule. Right: the confirm window at work — the “yes” is bound to one specific booking (cancel_target_id) and is valid only on the immediately next turn (confirm_turn); any other reply closes the window and the booking stands. Nothing is ever cancelled by accident.

Cancel

SCREEN-INVENTORY §2.5 · W19–W27

The safety mechanic is invisible but shapes everything here: the “yes” binds to cancel_target_id (the exact record, found by sender_key, never by a customer-supplied id) and is honoured only on the immediately next turn (confirm_turn). Any other message closes the window — W22.

Reschedule

SCREEN-INVENTORY §2.6 · W28–W39

Find the nearest upcoming booking, ask for the new slot, confirm, move. The cutoff applies to the old booking; the same one-turn confirm window applies to the “yes”; and a successful move resets the reminder, so the customer is reminded again for the new time.

New-slot rejections — straight to a human

SCREEN-INVENTORY §2.6 · W62–W65 · new (BULGU-9)

The engine had these four literals all along; the inventory's first edition missed them (BULGU-9, closed 2026-08-31). Deliberate asymmetry with booking: where W14–W18 loop back and re-ask, a rejected reschedule slot goes straight to handoff — the engine's own comment: busy/past/closed/invalid → handoff with context. A full engine sweep found no other missed primary literal.
